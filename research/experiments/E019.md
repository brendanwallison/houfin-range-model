# E019 How fast does DESK's backcast degrade with distance from its training years?
hypotheses: [S1, S3, T9, E3]     parents: [E004, E016]     eval_version: pre-v1     cost: ~5 min cpu
pre-registered: development run first (2026-10-09, r24, numbers below); the job re-runs it at r12/24/64.

design: research/exp/reach_curve.py. Four DESK models sharing the same 532 held-out cells (base: all years; tempho
models trained from 1976 / 1986 / 1996). 6-year windows from 1966; the observed community per held-out cell and window
(two ABBA halves, >= 4 surveyed years) projected into ESK; each model's z averaged over the same rows. Against the
cell's modern epoch (2005-2025), centered, noise-free by halves: corr, calibration (truth on DESK), mse_ratio
(E|dD - dT|^2 / E|dT|^2; < 1 = closer to the truth than no change), z_error (E|zD - zT|^2, uncentered).
reach = first training year - window centre.

reading (UNVERIFIED): a clean, monotone degradation with reach for the same target: correlation falls ~0.1 per 10
years, calibration from ~0.6 to 0.2, and beyond ~10 years of reach DESK's backcast state is FURTHER from the truth than
the modern state (mse_ratio 1.1-1.2, CIs above 1 from +15 y). Even in trained years DESK is only ~10% better than no
change in composition space. 1902-1939 lies 26-64 years before the BBS start; at the measured edge (+28 y) DESK's
temporal deviation already costs more than it carries -- for the age model, DESK's pre-BBS deviations need heavy
shrinkage plus an honest error term of at least ~0.15 (r24 units), and the production model (60 training years, vs
30-50 here) may do somewhat better. Beyond +28 years is extrapolation.
