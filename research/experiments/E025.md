# E025 Does DESK's backcast change between its selected epoch and the end of training?
hypotheses: [T1, T10, S1]     parents: [E022, E024, E019, E023]     eval_version: pre-v1     cost: 7 caches (~2 min each, cpu) + span_reach/reach_curve/atlas (~30 min cpu)
pre-registered: 2026-10-09 ~22:45, after reading the training logs (below), before any late-epoch model was graded.

question: the current-code tempho retrains backcast ~50% better than the archived tempho models (E019b, E023b). Every
selection signal is spatial (val_kernel on held-out cells), and the selected epoch of the HEAD runs is 65-162 out of
500. Does the model's temporal extrapolation keep changing after that epoch -- eroding (overfitting temporal structure)
or improving (still learning it)? If it moves a lot, the epoch chosen by a spatial metric is a lever on backcasting that
nobody selects for, and epoch differences are a candidate explanation for archived vs current.

already seen (rung 0, the runs' own train_trajectory.jsonl; single-year targets, so noisy and uncorrected): from the
selected epoch to epoch 490, the 1975->(last trained year) withheld pair's direction cosine (dcos_withheld) is flat to
rising in all 7 runs (t1985 s1 0.341 -> 0.372, s2 0.357 -> 0.380; t1995 s1 0.268 -> 0.278, s2 0.276 -> 0.276; spans
1976 0.170 -> 0.238, 1986 0.284 -> 0.309, 1996 0.227 -> ~0.26), while the rotation ratio roughly doubles (t1995 s1
0.29 -> 0.50; span 1976 0.07 -> 0.22). val_kernel (spatial) is 17-50% worse at 490 than at the selected epoch, but the
selected value is the argmin of a series that swings 10-30% between nearby epochs. Selecting on val_zmse (the archived
default) would have picked epochs 65-95 with the same direction and smaller rotation.

design: for each HEAD DESK run (E022 t1985 s1/s2, t1995 s1/s2; E024 spans 1976-1995, 1986-2005, 1996-2015) build a
second research cache from the run's last checkpoint (resume_checkpoint.pt.completed, epoch 490: model + output EMA;
everything else -- covariate normalization, basis, split -- identical). research/exp/epoch_variant.py; the source run is
not touched. Grade selected vs late with the same code on the same held-out cells:
- span_reach.py (E023b): raw and recalibrated backcast of every pre-T0 window against the first ten trained years;
- reach_curve.py (E019b): 1966-71 ... windows vs the modern epoch, t1995/t1985 s1/s2 selected and late;
- atlas.py --population withheld for t1995 s1/s2 selected and late: centered corr and the calibration slope
  (truth on DESK) -- the magnitude half.

predictions (rivals):
- erosion (longer training overfits temporal structure): late < selected by more than the seed spread (0.02-0.03 in
  corr) in >= 4 of the 7 runs at the farthest window.
- still learning (the logs' direction cosine): late >= selected; the calibration slope truth-on-DESK falls toward 1
  or below as movement doubles (DESK moves more for the same direction accuracy).
- null: within the seed spread in most runs; the epoch is not a lever on backcast skill.
decision rule: late better in >= 4 of 7 by more than the seed spread -> the spatial selection metric is costing
temporal skill: pre-register an epoch-selection test on a trained decade held back from selection only (no new
training needed beyond what E026 adds), and rerun the downstream checks (E013/E018) on late states. Late worse ->
early stopping is protecting backcasts; any temporal-supervision change must keep that guard. Null -> epoch selection
is not why archived models backcast worse (E026 tests the metric-pair count, the other recorded difference).
note (added before any result): this also bears directly on PRODUCTION. The production DESK (encoder/desk, 2026-09-14)
trained with holdout_frac 0, so it had no validation set and desk_training kept its FINAL weights (the "best is None"
branch). Every graded tempho/base model, by contrast, is a selected-epoch state. The late states are the ones that
correspond to what production ships.

Pre-registered rule: late better by more than the seed spread at the farthest window in 4 of 7 (t1985 s1/s2, spans
1976 and 1986) -> MET, at the threshold. "Erosion" refuted; "still learning" holds for DIRECTION, not for magnitude.
READING (UNVERIFIED): after the epoch the spatial criterion picks, DESK keeps improving the direction of its temporal
change (+0.01 to +0.08 in backcast corr; most at T0 1986 and for the badly-selected 1976 span model) while its
movement keeps growing faster than its accuracy, so squared-error skill against no change worsens, and its spatial
fidelity on held-out cells drops. Epoch selection trades temporal direction against magnitude and spatial fit.
Consequences: (1) PRODUCTION ships final-epoch weights: its backcast direction is somewhat better, and its
over-movement worse, than every graded (selected-epoch) model; the downstream readout should expect over-movement.
(2) E024's span comparison must be re-read at matched training state (E024b first look, late states).
(3) Next, per the rule: an epoch-selection test on a trained decade held back from selection; and the downstream
checks (E013 readout corr, E018 honesty) on late states.
