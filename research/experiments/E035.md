## E035b Which part of DESK's backcast change tracks the real change? (the skeptic's test; pre-registered 2026-10-10
## ~12:54, before running)
design: research/exp/backcast_attribution.py on tempho1995 s1 (selected) and its epoch-490 state: held-out cells, early
withheld epoch 1966-86 vs 2005-25; the early epoch re-encoded with land use (LUH, HYDE, BUI) or climate swapped year by
year for its value 39 years later. Land-use part = change minus change(land use swapped); climate part likewise. Each
graded against the observed community change (noise-free, centred): corr, k, size share; and its alignment with the
analog direction, with a random-rotation null (M19).
considerations: M1, M2, M19 (spatially aware null), M22 (the 1966-86 window is mid-ramp, not the plateau), T9, G2/G5
(direction vs magnitude; shrinking would not fix a wrong-direction part), G3.
predictions (the skeptic's hypothesis): the land-use part carries most of the size and most of whatever tracks the real
change; the climate part is small, aligns with the analogs and carries little or negative correlation with the truth.
Rival: both parts track the truth weakly and equally (the covariate families are not where the error lives).
reading: if the climate part's corr with the truth is <= 0 while it aligns with the analogs beyond the null, the
space-for-time error lives in how DESK reads climate change through spatial climate gradients -- a specific,
testable target (e.g. a DESK whose climate input is the BBS-era climate field, held fixed in a backcast).

Pre-registered: the skeptic's hypothesis CONFIRMED in both states -- the land-use-driven part is most of DESK's change
and carries ALL of its correlation with the real change, with no lean toward analogs; the climate-driven part tracks
nothing (corr 0.03-0.05) and carries the whole space-for-time lean (+0.25 to +0.31, far beyond its null).
WHY (established for one model, two states): DESK reads a place's climate change through spatial climate gradients --
it moves the community toward places that have that climate today -- and over 1966-2025 real communities did not
respond to climate change that way (or not on this timescale). Its land-use reading moves communities in a direction
that matches what happened (still over-scaled, k 0.3-0.4). Not yet separated: whether the land-use signal is a place's
own land-use change or a regional land-use trend read through position (E035 skeptic: production's land-use-driven
shift is mostly continent-wide).
Consequence (a testable target, not a fix yet, G7): a backcast in which DESK's CLIMATE input does not drive change --
e.g. climate held at its BBS-era field while land use varies -- should keep DESK's real temporal signal and drop its
space-for-time error. Before any such design: replicate on a second model/seed and on tempho1985, and check the
species-level readout of each part.
