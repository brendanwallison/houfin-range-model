NOTE (2026-10-11 21:55, the user, before any E055 result): the "little consistent temporal signal" argument above
depends on the span. Sampling noise is fixed per cell-year, while true change grows with the lag; the 40-year change
is ~100 km of structured turnover. A T0 1996 model trains on 30 years, without the 1966-95 half that carries the
longest-lag signal. So E055d tests the WEAKER case, and a null there does not speak for a model trained on all of
BBS (60 years). That case cannot be validated inside BBS, which needs a deeper external series (CBC, discussed with
the user) or a span-dose test.

WHAT IT SHOWS (one seed; the shape is the finding, steering):
- Space peaks EARLY (epoch ~70) and declines steadily as training over-fits (-0.032 by epoch 490).
- Time rises MONOTONICALLY through all 500 epochs, steeply to ~160 and slowly after, both raw (0.142 -> 0.264) and
  beyond the trend placebo (+0.033 from epoch 40 to 490).
- The spatial selection rule stops at epoch 162, where space has already fallen 0.009 from its peak and time is
  still climbing.
- Space and time are traded along ONE training run. The "over-fitting" regime is also where DESK keeps learning
  temporal structure.
WHY (likely, not tested):
- Learning order. The large, high-variance spatial structure is fitted first. The small temporal response directions
  (~6% of target variance) are fitted slowly, by the same capacity that also starts memorizing places.
- That fits E054 (less capacity: better space, worse time) and the seed ensemble (keeps the capacity, averages away
  the seed-specific memorization).
WHY weight averaging fails here (likely): snapshots within one run differ mainly by systematic training progress, not
by jitter around a basin. Their average scores like the middle snapshot (SWA 100-160 ~ epoch 130; SWA 300-490 ~
epoch 490). Seeds over-fit DIFFERENTLY, so averaging seeds cancels something; averaging one run's snapshots does not.
IMPLICATIONS (production, not decisions):
- Selecting the epoch on spatial validation picks a temporally under-trained state; nothing in production selects
  for time.
- A late-state seed ensemble gets most of the time gain while recovering part of the space loss.
- Whether time keeps rising beyond 500 epochs is E055d's question.
