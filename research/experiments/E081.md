READING (pre-registered): A HARM (mean -0.19, both intervals below 0, >= 0.12) and B HARM (mean -0.18, both below 0)
-> the harm to sedentary dev species' breeding-season change where cover changed most comes from supervising z with
WINTER (CBC) communities ITSELF -- not from the presence form, and not from the early lists.
WHAT DIFFERS between the forms: the Ruzicka term does not enlarge DESK's change overall (x0.95-1.02) and does not harm
the community sedentary species (+0.01 / +0.02; their predicted change even shrinks, SD 0.78); yet the readout of the
OUT-OF-COMMUNITY sedentary species is enlarged (SD 1.18-1.25) and its change worsens. So for both forms the harm falls
mainly on species the term does not see -- the coupling half of E078b, now dominant.
WHY (likely). Winter similarity structure among residents differs from breeding structure (season link ~0.5 for change,
E066c; worst in built-up cells, E079). Fitting z to winter structure moves it along directions the breeding readouts of
OTHER species pick up as extra change. The terms' own species can absorb the fit (their readout is part of the
calibration / the term), the dev species cannot. Not shown: which winter-breeding differences carry it.
IMPLICATION (production, reported; important): the dev sedentary species are the closest analogue of the House Finch --
an out-of-community resident. Supervising the current breeding-season representation with CBC winter data, in ANY form
tried (presence, Ruzicka, all years or 1966+), worsens breeding-season change for exactly that class of species where
cover changed most. CBC supervision should not be added to this representation for the House Finch backcast. It would
need a representation whose season matches (G11) or a target set that includes the focal class.
