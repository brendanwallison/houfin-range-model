## The main interpretation

**The relevant cross-model result is partial conservation of realized growth, not general process invariance.** The [cross-model process audit](CROSS_MODEL_PROCESS_AUDIT_20261101.md) compares all 59 locally checked, standard-yearling model versions against a common reference and adds matched structural contrasts at identical locations and eras. It covers Allee law, DESK basis, native departure, journey penalties, reproductive observations/conversion and initialization treatments, rather than restricting the inference to founder counts. Older unsupported/different-life-cycle versions remain outside this numeric comparison.

The illustrative comparisons now use a uniform regional order, **East, Great Plains, West**, and the same continuous metrics in every row: mean absolute change in C, potential lambda and actual-state local replacement. A second table applies the same symmetric relative-difference formula to every vital rate and aggregate. Great Plains refers to the primary 100W–95W band (189 fixed-observed cells), with East 206 and West 704. In the Plains, the annual-conversion/Allee-law/later-native-departure contrasts respectively give C changes .028/.046/.107, potential-lambda changes .069/.576/.219, and local-replacement changes .042/.055/.098. No inference should depend on switching metric or omitting the Plains.

On the same 206 eastern cells with positive modern BBS records, lowering annual nest conversion produces mean symmetric relative changes of 23.2% in juvenile survival and 24.6% in realized reproduction, but 3.5% in Runge C. Potential lambda is also relatively conserved (mean 2.222 to 2.249). By contrast, relative-to-absolute Allee changes mean potential lambda from 3.246 to 2.222 while mean absolute C and actual-state local-replacement changes are each .050. This demonstrates conservation of fitted-state replacement more strongly than conservation of growth capacity. The movement contrast is less stable: adding native departure in the later-state middle-abundance fit gives western mean absolute C/local-replacement changes .133/.152, with 50/704 fixed-observed western cells reversing beyond C=.95/1.05. These examples use equal observed-cell weights, not repeated-record weights.

Thus some structural changes do mainly redistribute demographic components while preserving realized growth; others change growth capacity or realized spatial contribution substantially. No claim of a structurally identified growth law follows from current fitted-state comparisons. Common-state growth responses and uncertainty must be examined separately. Within-model historical change is not evidence for or against this cross-model compensation question; the earlier temporal tables address another diagnostic.

**Scope correction: the high stability figures do not establish a stable eastern source/sink regime across project versions or through history.** They primarily compare founder-abundance scenarios within a model structure and the same era. Correlation can remain high under a large upward shift in growth; it is not a measure of agreement in absolute growth levels. The [eastern regime audit](EASTERN_REGIME_AUDIT_20261101.md) now reports equal-land levels, exact source/sink counts and same-cell temporal transitions across the 14 fits participating in those comparisons. This still does not cover every older project version, and the particular previously observed cross-version wholesale reversal has not been independently reconstructed here.

For the later-state middle-abundance control, equal-land eastern potential lambda rises from .303 in 1951–1965 to 1.430 in 2010–2025; source cells increase from 31/438 to 184/438. There are 153 same-cell sink-to-source transitions, including 123 cells going from below .8 to above 1.2. The corresponding native-departure fit rises from .366 to 1.435, with 134 sink-to-source transitions (96 below .8 to above 1.2). Thus the historical opening is substantial even when neighboring founder scenarios agree closely. Modern eastern median potential lambda is only .563/.571 in those fits, so a mean above one does not mean the whole eastern landscape is a source. Runge C likewise changes through time, but on fitted densities and a different scale: 74/77 cells cross from deficit to surplus, with no below-.8 to above-1.2 transitions in these two cases. This is not a clearance of historical-growth or invasion-timing pathologies.

**Component sensitivity is not the same as process sensitivity.** A new [functional comparison](FUNCTIONAL_STABILITY_20261101.md) directly compares spatial contribution, actual-state local replacement, net flux and export across 14 completed contrasts, using common spatial/time support. For the 1951 abundance scenarios, modern breeding-contribution map correlations against the middle scenario are .997–.999 without native departure and .977–.994 with it. Mean absolute contribution changes are .006–.009 and .010–.021, respectively. These observed-habitat process maps are considerably more stable than the native fits' survival decomposition. Initial abundance is currently fixed in separate scenarios; these results do not measure uncertainty from a freely estimated abundance parameter.

**Mechanism changes can still change realized source/sink geography.** In the later-state middle-abundance comparison, adding native departure gives modern western contribution-map correlation .769, mean absolute change .121, and direct source/sink reversals over 10.7% of positive-record weight (14.0% in 1966–1985). The categories use C<.95, C>1.05 and a neutral band. This is a material spatial difference despite western average C staying near one. Founder-bracket comparisons have no such modern reversals on positive-record support. Equal-land checks broadly preserve contribution stability, but expose weaker net-flux stability in the native low-abundance case. The full comparison therefore reports region, era, spatial weighting, continuous change and categorical change together.

These are cross-fit robustness results, not posterior identification certificates. Nevertheless, they are positive evidence that some realized processes can remain stable while demographic subcomponents compensate. Independent historical/abundance and vital-rate observations are needed for claims about initial abundance and separate survival/reproduction, and for testing extrapolation. Their absence does not establish that the entire fitted process is unconstrained. Ecological process robustness and biological mechanism credibility must both enter model selection.

**Potential-lambda stability is also high, but is a different statement.** On identical modern positive-BBS support, founder-bracket potential-lambda map correlations are .998–.999 for controls and .990–.995 with native departure, versus Runge C correlations .997–.999 and .977–.994. Exact threshold crossings at 1 occur in 15/38/93/34 cells for lambda and 27/39/75/53 for C (control low/high, native low/high, each versus middle; 1,689 supported cells). Thus neither classification is perfectly invariant, and neither uniformly has fewer crossing cells. Potential lambda changes more in absolute units (.085–.361 versus C .006–.021), but these diagnostics have different ranges and mechanisms, so this is not a calibrated comparison of relative uncertainty. Potential lambda here is Sa + Sj*Fmax before density/Allee suppression, density-dependent disease and movement losses; it is not actual growth at vanishing density under absolute Allee. With later-state native departure, western map correlation is .961 for potential lambda but .769 for C, illustrating how similar potential-growth geography can coexist with materially different realized contribution geography. Details and exact counts are in the functional comparison.

The most consistent explanation is a mismatch among **local replacement, the mechanism holding the Plains barrier, and slow eastern expansion**, with count observations allowing the model to trade reproduction, age-specific survival, movement, carrying capacity and habitat history. Float64 LBFGS can exploit these trades more thoroughly. Changing a mechanism often improves one part of the explanation and moves the demand elsewhere.

The evidence does not identify a single offending dispersal coefficient, show that the improved DESK basis is intrinsically harmful, or establish that all sinks should disappear. Nor does it support selecting the old absolute-Allee/native-departure fit from its favorable resident-survival and western fundamental-sink summaries. Its actual contribution and establishment mechanism must be considered together. The objective remains a parsimonious, biologically credible configuration that can also support posterior inference.

## What the structural experiments establish

**Yearling breeding is a necessary life-cycle correction.** It removes much of the extreme raw fecundity demand associated with delayed first breeding in older fits. Raw Fmax and local eigenvalues therefore cannot be compared across those life cycles without accounting for their definitions. A shared habitat field also removes some projection freedom. Neither correction eliminates compensation among demographic rates.

**The Allee law changes the demographic explanation substantially.** On the combined NestWatch/old-CBC-anchor reference, relative Allee gives modern population-weighted adult survival around .40 and resident Sj/Sa around .995; the absolute-law reference gives .56 and .48 with similar count prediction and realized annual nest-site output. This is real evidence of structural sensitivity. It does not by itself establish that the absolute law's fitted spatial mechanism is credible.

**Native departure principally changes retention and exchange.** The new origin-attributed diagnostics show the distinction:

| Reference configuration | West breeding contribution C | West retained contribution R | Western breeder weight in cells with C<.95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Absolute Allee, original initialization | .988 | .491 | 16.0% |
| + Native departure | .989 | .840 | 22.1% |
| DESK-v2 absolute + native departure | .990 | .835 | 22.9% |
| Later 1951 middle-abundance control | .988 | .483 | 16.7% |
| Later 1951 middle-abundance + native departure | .990 | .839 | 22.6% |

These are summaries over fitted modern origins, not independent measurements. A regional mean near one is unsurprising for a slowly changing fitted population and can hide cell heterogeneity. Native departure clearly changes retention/export; its previously reported reduction in fundamental-sink occupancy cannot be translated into elimination of actual demographic deficits. Conversely, these contribution summaries do not invalidate a biologically plausible native-retention mechanism. They specify what it actually does in the fitted explanation.

The 2010–2025 regional isolation replays leave roughly 98–99% of fitted final western abundance when outside-west immigration is removed in these cases. Thus much of the exchange sustaining individual western cells is internal redistribution, not a demonstrated requirement for a large subsidy from beyond the region. Gross cell immigration must not be called external regional immigration. The full budgets and deficit distributions, including other thresholds, remain available in the appendix.

**Additional journey penalties have not yet earned a clear role.** Distance-hazard additions generally offer little predictive gain; their fitted magnitude can vary strongly across seeds when combined with native departure. Path compounding became interpretable after correcting the omitted land-share floor in its features, but the completed comparisons do not show a consistent incremental advantage over simpler movement configurations. These findings support keeping movement complexity restrained while evaluating the actual origin/destination flows.

**DESK-v2 changes a tradeoff, rather than simply improving or worsening everything.** It can reduce the positive-growth/K–Allee nonviability diagnostic at observed locations and improve prediction. In one absolute/native seed it also restores near-ceiling juvenile survival. Better ecological representation remains useful; the demographic explanation still needs independent constraints and seed checks.

## The Plains barrier: mechanism, not just prevalence

The [exact Plains C decomposition](PLAINS_C_DECOMPOSITION_20261101.md) explains why contribution is not invariant across refits. In the matched later-state middle-abundance control/native comparison, equal-land Plains mean C changes .651 to .765. Adult-survivor contribution is nearly unchanged (.199 to .201); offspring-recruit contribution increases .453 to .564. Retention is .482 to .487, successful export .170 to .278. Thus movement and vital-rate refitting alter realized recruit contribution, rather than simply reclassifying nominal local growth. Successful emigrants remain credited to their origin. Among the fits at the 65-91% sink-prevalence endpoints, mean absolute C change is .179; 102 cells cross 1 and 27 cross beyond .95/1.05, so threshold sensitivity alone does not explain the spread. Those extreme fits differ in several assumptions and are not a matched causal experiment.

The [source-connectivity audit](PLAINS_SOURCE_CONNECTIVITY_20261101.md) now directly tests majority-sink prevalence and connected source paths across all 59 checked yearling fits. In 2010–2025, primary Plains sink shares are 64.7–90.5% for Runge C and 88.4–98.4% for actual-state no-movement replacement, so both remain majority-sink in every fit. Potential-lambda sink shares range from 27.9–62.1%; only 21/59 fits remain majority-sink by that diagnostic. Coast-to-coast potential-lambda source paths exist in 38/59 modern era maps using shared edges (35/59 at lambda>1.05), or all 59 if diagonal contacts count. No coast-to-coast C or local-replacement path occurs in any tested era or annual map, including full-domain route sensitivity. Nevertheless, 58/59 modern fits have a narrow C-source path across the primary Plains band: majority-sink prevalence does not imply an unbroken sink barrier. Both primary and broader Plains masks in this connectivity audit are restricted to 25–49N; paths use grid adjacency rather than dispersal jumps. A [checked example path](SOURCE_BRIDGE_MAP_20261101.png) shows the later-state native-departure middle scenario. These are separate mechanisms, not interchangeable definitions of the Plains barrier.

The historical lack of west-to-east expansion makes an inhospitable Plains barrier reasonable. The specific concern is the model creating a persistent barrier from **positive potential growth plus K so small that absolute mate limitation prevents self-replacement at every density**. This differs from intrinsically unfavorable habitat and from viable habitat temporarily below an establishment threshold.

The saved Allee-dead mask already selects that positive-growth/no-positive-equilibrium category. Local finite-propagule replays now confirm deterministic failure of those classified cells across the tested seed sizes, while many viable cells depend on initial density. These are tests of the fitted deterministic mechanism, not biological validation of it or estimates of stochastic establishment probability. Within-cell clustering, finite colonists and the meaning of the absolute density scale remain important.

In the original absolute/native seeds, the narrow Plains band has 4.9–11.7% of cells in that category, containing 5.3–9.5% of positive modern BBS records. The question is whether these locations are being made sinks through a credible demographic mechanism, not whether reducing every barrier cell is desirable. Potential growth, viability, actual contribution, immigration dependence and spatial connectivity answer different questions and must remain separate.

Actual successful permanent movements are now measured rather than inferred from nominal kernel bands. Across several current controls, mean successful western off-cell displacement is about 215–259 km, with substantial long-distance tails. These are conditional on surviving movement and recruitment; they are not movements of all birds or seasonal migration estimates. They need an independent permanent-movement comparison before being called biologically appropriate. Possible conflation of seasonal displacement with permanent dispersal remains a hypothesis to check, not an established explanation.

## Later initialization: a useful change that exposes compensation

The [temporal functional comparison](TEMPORAL_FUNCTIONAL_STABILITY_20261101.md) now covers 1951–1965, 1966–1985, 1986–2009 and 2010–2025 on identical modern-positive BBS cell support. Founder-scenario contribution-map correlations remain .993–.994 (control) and .989–.994 (native departure) in 1951–1965; potential-lambda correlations are .997–.998 and .988–.998. Stability therefore is not restricted to the modern era. Native-scenario mean absolute C differences are larger early (.025–.039 in 1951–1965, .024–.042 in 1966–1985) than modern (.010–.021). Potential-lambda magnitude sensitivity, conversely, grows toward the modern era in these contrasts. These are era-aggregated, fitted-process robustness checks on later surveyed habitat, not pre-BBS validation or evidence that the early expansion/Allee mechanisms are biologically correct. Exact crossings of 1 occur in all eras; no inference rests solely on map correlation.

All six seed-41 1951 established-state fits have completed. They initialize the release-cell population after local establishment, remove the ten-year Allee exemption and replace the old backcast pin with fixed broad abundance scenarios. The conditional 280/560/1,120 bird-equivalents are not observed censuses or uncertainty intervals. The inherited equal adult/young split and within-cell spatial support remain assumptions. No historical CBC likelihood was added or double-counted.

The new controls markedly reduce the low-K/absolute-Allee symptom: Plains nonviable-cell shares are approximately 0–.42%, versus 1.27% in the old absolute control. Their positive-observation context also improves. But resident juvenile/adult survival returns to .977–.980, with roughly 66–69% of fitted modern population above .95. Native-departure versions have fewer of these ceiling problems in some abundance brackets, but their adult/juvenile survival balance varies materially with the initialized abundance.

This is not a reason to restore the biologically questionable release exemption. It shows how much that early treatment affected the later demographic explanation. The later-state premise remains worth pursuing; its current fits have not yet reduced the joint pathology set convincingly. Two cold seed-42 middle-abundance replicates and two matched later-state no-NestWatch fits are queued to separate optimizer-basin sensitivity from initialization/reproductive-observation interaction.

Fitted arrival medians remain about three years early. Frozen-release errors remain roughly 28–31 years early in the later-state fits. Part of the change from the old 36–38-year errors is simply the eleven-year-later start. It cannot alone establish that growth/dispersal has become more credible. Early-to-modern habitat-growth changes also persist.

## How NestWatch and the other observations fit this picture

**NestWatch constrains realized reproduction, not Fmax alone.** At the same 1,089 eligible nest cell-years, the original absolute control predicts annual output around 3.57–3.58 with nests, versus 3.34–3.38 in the completed no-nest controls. Yet Fmax is lower with nests. This demonstrates that density limitation, survival, habitat allocation and annual conversion mediate the effect. On this absolute-law/original-initialization comparison, near-ceiling juvenile survival is absent even without NestWatch; the healthy ratio cannot be attributed solely to the nest likelihood. The native-departure ablations and later-state ablations remain unfinished.

The reported attempts do not identify complete annual attempts per female or breeding participation. The existing annual-conversion sensitivity already moves demographic allocations considerably. Nest information remains valuable; its conversion uncertainty must be represented rather than assuming that per-attempt observations directly measure annual population fecundity. The old synthetic mean/centering contract issue also remains a separate numerical/design repair; it should not be treated as a clean identification certificate or as evidence that real nesting data are unhelpful.

**CBC and eBird preparation/calibration are complete, but a full joint demographic fit is not.** CBC supplies 106,894 circle-years at 4,256 circles, with historical effort and preferential early sampling requiring explicit treatment. Raw focal eBird extraction supplies complete-checklist denominators in the US and Canada; source baselines and the first fixed-population Canadian breeding comparison have run. The full-count NB2 control passes its numerical checks, but recent Canadian detections are underpredicted, approximately 3.1% versus 4.84% observed.

That mismatch could reflect population predictions, observation-effort calibration, census timing or remaining sampling differences. It is not evidence for a particular demographic parameter change. The existing BBS census mapping has been reproduced exactly, but exact software parity does not validate its biological correspondence to June eBird or winter CBC. Standard shared-state, source-specific observation models remain the appropriate direction. Northeast partial migration calls for a defensible seasonal observation mapping and sensitivity analysis, rather than silently treating winter observations as sedentary breeding populations. A fully dynamic migration model is not required by this work.

**BBL has independent survival information, but the full spatial likelihood is unfinished.** The adult inventory contains 296,896 bands and 782 dead recoveries. The coarse reporting-aware survival block and the exact adult movement/destination-survival primitive have been implemented/tested. Full cohort propagation, partial exposure, reporting and recovery-location handling remain necessary before incorporating it into the spatial population likelihood. Coarse regional survival estimates must not be imposed as tight priors on differently weighted model summaries. Seasonal movement records also need classification before serving as permanent-dispersal evidence.

## HMC feasibility and parsimony

The additional computational audit reuses the actual unconstrained posterior with nominal priors and Jacobians. Its 19,275 coordinates are mostly observer nuisance effects, so parameter count alone does not describe ecological complexity or sampler difficulty. Matrix-free curvature/roughness probes retain those nuisance effects; they are not a conditional Hessian with observers fixed.

The full comparisons remain queued. One diagonal NUTS run supplies timing calibration before cost-supported comparisons are released. Warmup and sampling now checkpoint with adaptation and RNG preserved; exact interruption/resume tests pass. The measured CPU gradient time implies tens of hours for depth-saturated screens, so a six-hour attempt limit is not a completion estimate. Geometry is explanatory evidence; actual mixing/energy behavior and effective sampling per unit computation determine feasibility. The smoke test is not an HMC-readiness result. ## Working direction and limits

Retain the corrected yearling life cycle and parsimonious shared-field baseline; pursue the established-state initialization rather than ranking the artificial early exemption as a solution. Keep absolute Allee and native departure as explicit competing mechanisms whose benefits and costs are measured together. The current evidence does not justify adding several more movement scalars or declaring one configuration the winner.

The immediate discriminating work is already executing: matched initialization/seed/nesting comparisons, full origin-attributed demographic budgets, successful permanent distances and local establishment checks, and computational feasibility probes. The data-integration work must then constrain the particular unresolved components—realized reproduction and annual conversion, age-specific survival, survey abundance/census correspondence and permanent movement—rather than merely increasing the volume of count likelihood. Historical CBC should constrain the modeled historical observations without reusing the same evidence as an independent founder backcast.

The complete P1–P15 assessment remains linked above, including the still-incomplete projection/extrapolation, field-scale and departure-map interpretation. No missing diagnostic has been converted into a pass. The main conclusion is that we now see **where improvements are being paid for elsewhere**, and can target those substitutions with matched experiments and independent observations, rather than adopting a slightly better fit with a different hidden pathology.
