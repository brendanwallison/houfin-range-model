# E054 Does reducing DESK's over-fitting change its temporal extrapolation?
hypotheses: [T1, T3, T11]     parents: [E022, E026, E039i-o, E046]     eval_version: pre-v1
cost: 2 x ~60 min gpu + ~15 min cpu
pre-registered: 2026-10-11 21:36, before either model exists. Prompted by the user: "doesn't this all signal that we
are in an over-fitting regime, and that is one of our concerns?"

background (training logs; steers):
- Held-out-place error bottoms at epochs ~80-110, then rises 15-19% while training error falls. The selected epochs
  (100-165) are already past that minimum. The held-out/train gap is 1.5x at the selected phase and 2.2x at epoch 500.
- The archived sweep (no temporal holdout, one seed each):
  - dropout 0.05/0.2 and weight decay 1e-3 leave the late gap unchanged (1.97-2.01x vs base 1.99x);
  - hidden width 64 (w64) cuts it to 1.60x, and a heavier metric weight (mw20-100) to 1.47-1.75x, at equal best
    spatial validation.
  - None changed held-out cells' 1996->2025 direction cosine in TRAINED years (+0.21-0.23).
  - Whether less over-fitting helps in WITHHELD decades was never tested.
design:
- T0 1996 and T0 1986, seed 1, hidden_width 64 (production 128); all else identical to E022 seed 1 (split, seed,
  selection).
- Caches at the selected and late (epoch 490) states. Verify keys/split equal to the control; copy esk files.
- Evaluation: fair_rivals --incremental --n-pos 96 --pos-seeds 1-5 --strong-base --ref-cache <E022 seed 1>, paired.
  - (a) raw backcast change;
  - (c) beyond the placebo;
  - (d) beyond the strong base;
  - space_level (held-out spatial skill).
  - Plus the training-log gap.
- Seed-noise reference: E026b's 65k seed 2 vs seed 1 difference on the same quantities (T0 1996).
predictions:
- If over-fitting limits temporal extrapolation: w64 improves (a) or (c) by >= +0.02 at the SELECTED state at both
  T0s, with its late-state gap smaller than the control's.
- If not: (a)/(c) within +-0.02 of the control while the gap shrinks.
- Either way: space_level within ~0.01 of the control at the selected state; at the late state w64 >= control (less
  over-fitting).
decision rule:
- LESS OVER-FITTING HELPS TIME: (a) >= +0.02 with CI > 0 at the selected state at BOTH T0s.
  - Then: seeds 2-3 and the mw40 arm before anything is called a finding.
  - A production change would be an escalation, reported as an implication.
- NO TEMPORAL EFFECT: (a) CI includes 0 at both T0s, AND the late-state gap is smaller (so the manipulation worked).
- MANIPULATION FAILED: the late gap is not smaller. Then this run says nothing about over-fitting.
- Otherwise: mixed, report sizes.
considerations: M28 (one yardstick, paired), G6/M9 (selected and late), M11/M20 (adequate placebo).
