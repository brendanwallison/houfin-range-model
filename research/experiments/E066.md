# E066 Can early CBC supervise DESK's Ruzicka space through an observation model? Two preconditions
# (pre-registered 2026-10-13, before any run; no smoke -- running the script IS the test)
hypotheses: [CBC (bridge / complement)]     parents: [E065, plans/raw_surveys.md (section 7), G11, M30]
eval_version: pre-v1     cost: ~2 x 10 min cpu
why: the user (2026-10-13) asked whether DESK could keep predicting in Ruzicka space for the early years while the loss
is evaluated in some other space. Principled forms all put an OBSERVATION MODEL between the prediction and the early
data, inside DESK's pairwise metric loss (dot(z_i, z_j) ~ Ruzicka, desk_training.py _pair_kernel_loss): (1) ORDINAL --
trust only the order of early similarities within bins of matched completeness (non-metric MDS stress, Kruskal 1964;
the post-1966 metric loss keeps the scale); (2) CALIBRATED forward model in similarity space, fitted on the
pseudo-early copies (E065), which needs the degradation mechanism to be right; (3) species-level observation model with
a z -> species decoder and a per-count effort latent. Rejected: an effort-invariant similarity for early pairs (e.g.
Ruzicka on proportions), because no such similarity is a function of dot(z_i, z_j) under the kernel contract.
Identifiability (stated before data): an ordinal loss on early-modern pairs can supply WHERE change was larger or
smaller, not the ERA-WIDE amount, which is confounded with era-wide incompleteness; only (2), or DESK's covariates, can.
Before any DESK run, two things must hold, and both can be checked without training:
T1 SEASON LINK -- z lives in BBS (breeding) Ruzicka space; early CBC is a winter community. In modern data, does CBC
  similarity order pairs as BBS similarity does? (G11.)
T2 ORDER UNDER DEGRADATION -- in the pseudo-early copies (1966-85 counts degraded to 1920-39 completeness, thinning
  or omission), does observed similarity keep the order of the true similarity within completeness bins?
design (research/exp/e066_similarity_links.py; fixed now): Ruzicka on log1p counts over the 83 reference species that
survive CBC name harmonization (13 of 96 dropped as split-affected), same 83 on the BBS side.
- T1: cell-years with both BBS (route mean, the research cache desk_tempho_1995_s1) and CBC (mean over the cell's
  circles; counts standardized to median party-hours, exponent 0.8 -- primary; raw reported), 1966-2024, winter ending
  in the BBS year. 100k pairs each: SPATIAL (two cells, one year), TEMPORAL (one cell, >= 10 years apart), SPACETIME
  (two cells, >= 5 years apart). Every pair is replicated one year later, giving ABBA cross-season Spearman,
  disattenuated by the square roots of the BBS-BBS and CBC-CBC replicate Spearmans.
- T2: per mechanism, 100k pairs each: EARLY-EARLY (two degraded counts, different circles), EARLY-MODERN (degraded vs a
  2005-24 count of another circle), SAME-CIRCLE CROSS-ERA (degraded vs the same circle's 2005-24 count). Completeness =
  benchmark completeness relative to the circle's own 1986+ mean (M30), tertile bins. Spearman observed vs true,
  pooled and within bins (weighted mean and minimum over bins with >= 200 pairs); median shift observed - true per bin.
reading (fixed now):
- T1 (standardized): PASS if disattenuated Spearman >= 0.7 SPATIAL and >= 0.5 TEMPORAL; FAIL if < 0.4 spatial or < 0.3
  temporal; else PARTIAL. Disattenuated values are read only where both replicate Spearmans are >= 0.3 (else: raw
  ABBA reported, "not disattenuable"). T1 is CONSERVATIVE: a CBC circle (~24 km) and BBS routes in the same cell sample
  different ground, a systematic mismatch disattenuation does not remove.
- T2 (per mechanism): ORDER KEPT if within-bin Spearman >= 0.8 for early-early and early-modern AND >= 0.6 for
  same-circle cross-era. Binning matters if pooled is >= 0.05 below within-bin. Median shifts differing across bins by
  >= 0.05 = an offset an ordinal loss discards and only a calibrated forward model (2) could supply.
decision: T1 PASS and T2 order kept under both mechanisms -> design an ordinal early-CBC term for DESK's metric loss
(E067; yardstick: readout generalization to held-out places and to the early era, community and dev-species targets,
as E060/E061). T2 kept under one mechanism only -> decided by E065's scenario-realism readout (F). T1 FAIL (temporal)
-> winter change does not order like breeding change: early CBC cannot supervise the breeding-season z through any
loss, and the question becomes G11 (a winter representation), not loss design. T1 PARTIAL -> report; restrict to
resident species as an exploratory follow-up (G11), proxy species only.
considerations: G11 (season; never House Finch data -- not among the reference species, asserted), M30 (site-relative
completeness), M28 (one yardstick per claim), M2/M13 (point values with their n; skeptic round before any finding),
M29 (Audubon's curators start in 1966: T1 uses only 1966+; T2 uses modern data degraded, so neither tests real early
counts directly -- E065's split-half and artifact checks do).

DECISION (pre-registered): the conditions for E067 (T1 pass AND T2 kept) are not met -> no ordinal early-CBC term on
SINGLE COUNTS.
WHY (T2: likely). At ~0.3 relative completeness a single count records about a third of what the site holds, so which
species happen to be listed dominates the similarity of two counts; the true similarity's rank survives at 0.3-0.6
and the temporal (same-circle) signal survives worst, because the true variation among a circle's own decades is small
relative to the sampling noise added. Not yet shown: whether AVERAGING several degraded counts (DESK's targets are
route means; early communities could be decade means) restores the order -- E066b.
WHY (T1 temporal: unknown). Candidates: winter-specific dynamics (waterfowl, irruptive finches, feeder species), the
northward shift of winter ranges documented from CBC data, and the circle-vs-route ground mismatch. T1 is conservative
by design (stated above), so 0.45 is a lower bound on the season link for change.

## E066b Does averaging several degraded counts restore the order? (pre-registered 2026-10-13 ~17:25, before running)
why: E066 T2 failed for SINGLE counts; DESK's targets are already averages (BBS route means per cell-year), and an early
community could be a circle's decade mean. If averaging k counts restores the order at a k that early circles
actually have, early CBC can still supply an ordinal (or calibrated) term at that grain.
design (e066_similarity_links.py t2 --agg 1 3 5 10; same copies, species, completeness bins): per circle and era, one
UNIT = the mean of k random counts of that circle in the era (early 1966-85 degraded; modern 2005-24), circles with
>= k counts there; pairs among units as in T2 (bins with >= 100 pairs; same-circle pairs = one per circle, so their n
is the number of circles). k = 1 re-runs T2 at the unit grain for comparison. Also reported: what real pre-1940 data
offers -- counts per circle per decade (median, share >= 3, >= 5).
reading: per mechanism, the smallest k with within-bin Spearman >= 0.8 (early-early, early-modern) and >= 0.6
(same-circle cross-era) = the averaging an early term needs. USABLE GRAIN if that k is <= the median counts per circle
per decade before 1940 (else: only the share of circle-decades with >= k counts could contribute -- reported). No k
up to 10 keeps the order -> early CBC can enter only through a pooled model (E065's stratum indices), not through
pairs of early communities. Caveat: a 20-year degraded window mixes up to 20 years of true change into a unit, as a
real decade mean mixes 10.

Real pre-1940: 1,896 circle-decades, median 2 counts each; 35% have >= 3, 18% have >= 5 (holding 48% of the counts).
READING (pre-registered): OMISSION -- order restored at k = 5 (> the median of 2 per circle-decade: not a general
grain; the 18% of circle-decades with >= 5 counts, half of all early counts, could contribute). THINNING -- no k up to
10 restores early-modern or same-circle order (they stay ~0.6 and ~0.24 while early-early climbs to 0.87): early CBC
could enter only through a pooled model under that mechanism.
WHY (likely). Averaging removes NOISE, not BIAS. Under omission the damage is mostly noise (which species happened to be
listed), and the mean of 5 lists recovers the community. Under thinning to ~1% survival every degraded count is ~100x
smaller, a systematic scale gap to modern counts that log1p-Ruzicka reads as dissimilarity; early-early pairs share it
(so they recover) while early-modern and same-circle pairs do not. That gap is exactly what an effort term rescales
(E065) -- i.e. under thinning the observation model must include effort scaling, not only averaging.
WHICH MECHANISM real early counts resemble is not established: E065's scenario-realism readout (F) bears on it; the
earlier descriptive comparison (median non-zero count 6 early vs 13 at low modern effort) is far milder than ~1% survival
and leans toward omission-like incompleteness, likely mixed with genuinely smaller effort.
NOT TESTED (candidates, not pre-registered): averaging across neighbouring circles in a cell or stratum instead of
across years; 20-year early windows; rescaling degraded counts by an estimated effort before the similarity.
