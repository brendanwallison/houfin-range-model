# E080 Does LUH3 land use agree with the county agricultural census, 1929-1982?
# (pre-registered 2026-10-15, before running; the census files were inspected only for variable names)
hypotheses: [covariates (land use), cover extrapolation]     parents: [CONSIDERATIONS M31, E073 (peer), E075]
eval_version: pre-v1     cost: ~20 min cpu
why: M31 found LUH3's High Plains irrigation wrong in level and trend against USGS. The user asked whether land COVER
should also be replaced by CONUS-specific data. Before any replacement, measure LUH3 against the county census (the
historical record LUH3/HYDE downscale from).
data: ICPSR 35206 (Haines, Fishback & Rhode, United States Agriculture Data 1840-2012; user download, E:/Datasets/
ICPSR_agriculture). Census years 1929, 1939, 1949, 1959, 1969, 1982: harvested cropland and irrigated land per county,
as shares of county land area (variables in research/exp/e080_census_vs_luh3.py). LUH3: the covariates' raw yearly
states for the same years; cropland = sum of the five crop fractions; irrigated = sum of crop fraction x irrigated
fraction; county means on a 9x9 sub-grid of the 27-km cells. 2018 county boundaries (counties kept when the census
land area is within 10% of the polygon). Zones by majority area; GREAT PLAINS primary, west and east reported.
note on levels: LUH3 cropland includes idle and fallow land while the census measure is HARVESTED cropland, so levels
differ by definition; the tests use ranks and changes.
reading (Great Plains, cropland):
- LUH3 CROPLAND ADEQUATE if the level Spearman is >= 0.8 in every year AND the change Spearman is >= 0.6 for each of
  1929->1949, 1949->1969 and 1929->1982 -> keep LUH3 cover; replace only irrigation (and treat natural grassland /
  rangeland as bookkeeping).
- LUH3 CROPLAND INADEQUATE if the change Spearman is < 0.4 for any interval, or the level Spearman < 0.6 in any year ->
  county census cropland is a candidate replacement (a production decision for the user).
- Otherwise: report, by interval and zone.
Irrigation (all zones): the same statistics; M31 predicts poor agreement in the Great Plains (level and trend ratio).
FIX (2026-10-15, before any result; the first run crashed reading DS0019): the 1940 water part (DS0019, the 1929 and
1939 irrigated acres) has no FIPS column, only ICPSR state / county codes; it is mapped to FIPS through the 1940 main
part (DS0016). No statistic was produced by the crashed run.

## E080b (decided AFTER E080's result -- a follow-up, same bars): TOTAL cropland
E080 compared LUH3 with HARVESTED cropland. Acreage-diversion and conservation programs (Soil Bank 1956-60s, 1960s
diversion) idle cropland without changing it to another cover, so harvested cropland can fall while cropland does not.
E080b repeats the cropland comparison with TOTAL cropland (1929 DS0013 var12; 1939 DS0016 var10 + var12 + var14,
harvested + failed + idle / fallow; 1949 DS0021 var43; 1969 DS0033 item01036; 1982 DS0040 item01014; 1959 has no
total item and is dropped). Changes 1929->1949, 1949->1969, 1929->1982. Same reading rule as E080.

WHY the two readings differ (likely): harvested cropland fell with acreage-diversion and conservation programs (Soil
Bank, 1960s diversion: harvested x0.79 for 1949->69 while total x1.05) -- land idled, not converted; LUH3's crop
fractions follow total cropland. The east's low 1929->49 total-cropland agreement (0.12) probably reflects a definition
change (1949 "cropland, total" includes cropland used only for pasture; the 1930 total does not) -- not checked.
IRRIGATION: poor before 1950 (Great Plains level -0.07 / 0.17 in 1929 / 1939) and far too flat (x2.1 vs the census
x6.2, 1929-82), confirming M31 at county level.
IMPLICATION (production, reported): LUH3 CROPLAND is sound in where cropland is and in its regional decadal trend, with
moderate county-level change agreement (0.5-0.7 in the Great Plains) -- no strong case to replace it. LUH3 IRRIGATION is
not usable for the early Great Plains; county census irrigated acres (1929-1982, ~decadal) are a direct replacement
candidate. The decision is the user's.
