# E065 Can sampling-completeness covariates make raw CBC usable before 1940?
# (pre-registered 2026-10-13, before any full fit; a 20-step code smoke of every fit and the evaluation was run, its
# numbers not read as results)
hypotheses: [CBC (bridge / complement)]     parents: [E064, E064b, plans/raw_surveys.md (C1; section 7)]
eval_version: pre-v1     cost: 20 fits x ~25 min cpu (4 parallel, ~2.5 h) + ~5 min evaluation
why: the user (2026-10-13): "Going earlier than 1940 would be extremely helpful." E064b left ~0.19 of the pre-1940
held-out shortfall unexplained by density or by the number of field counters. Diagnosis since (cbc_completeness.py,
established descriptively): early counts at the SAME circle and the SAME number of field counters record far fewer
species (within 382 circles, x0.31 in 1901-19, x0.54 in 1920-39, x0.78 in 1940-65 relative to 1966+), and the median
share of a circle's modern benchmark species recorded is 0.14 / 0.26 / 0.57 / 0.87 (1901-19 / 1920-39 / 1940-65 /
1966+). Field counters therefore miss most of the early difference in effective effort. Uncorrected, this reads as a
spurious species-wide INCREASE out of the early era.
literature: list-length as an effort proxy for unstructured records (Szabo et al. 2010; Isaac et al. 2014); benchmark
species / local frequency standardization (Frescalo, Hill 2012; Pescott et al. on its link to occupancy-detection);
occupancy-detection with list-length detection covariates (Kery et al. 2010; van Strien et al. 2013); CBC effort as a
power function (Link & Sauer 1999).
covariates (computed from the lists, leave-one-out per species so a species' own record cannot predict itself;
research/exp/cbc_completeness.py -> completeness_v3.npz, benchmark sets from 1966+ counts):
- listlen: log(species recorded - own record);
- bench: logit of the share of the circle's BENCHMARK species (recorded on >= 50% of its 1966+ counts) recorded, own
  record removed. Benchmark completeness is robust to real changes in total richness; list length is not.
- RELATIVE TO THE SITE (user, 2026-10-13: "some areas are genuinely richer than others"): both are measured against the
  circle's OWN mean over its 1966+ fitted counts (--ref-from 1966), so 0 = "as complete as this site usually is in
  modern sampling", and the index is read there. Circles with < 3 modern counts (mostly early-only circles) borrow the
  nearest circle's reference within ~1 degree, else their stratum's median. Pre-1940 rows in circles with >= 5 modern
  counts: 34% (1901-19), 46% (1920-39) -- the rest rely on a neighbour's richness.
variants (research/exp/cbc_effort_model.py, counters effort, as E064 otherwise; 6,000 steps):
  V0 counters only (= E064's model)          V1 + listlen          V2 + bench          V3 = V2 as a DETECTION model
  (presence/absence, cloglog link; index = mean detection probability of the stratum's circles).
parts: full (all circles); h0 / h1 (two random circle halves, independent circles); thin / omit (pseudo-early copies).
PSEUDO-EARLY (known truth; research/exp/cbc_pseudo_early.py): copies of the raw request with every 1966-1985 count
degraded to the median 1920-39 benchmark completeness (0.26), severity log-normal across counts (sd 0.8), FIELD COUNTERS
UNCHANGED -- the early situation, in data with a known answer. Two mechanisms: thin = binomial thinning of individuals
(less effort; median survival 1.2%), omit = whole species records dropped regardless of count (incomplete reporting;
median retention 37%). Benchmark sets and site references from 1986+ for these fits (--ref-from 1986). Truth = the same
variant's fit to the real data; target = the 1966-85 -> 2005-24 change.
evaluation (research/exp/e065_eval.py; fixed now):
  A pseudo-early recovery: species-level median signed bias, median |bias|, corr; pooled stratum-level corr (species-
    centred). B split-half reliability of the change 1910-39 -> 1950-65 (h0 vs h1; raw and species-centred), with
    1967-86 -> 2005-24 for scale. C effort-artifact diagnostic: species-level change 1910-39 -> 1950-65 (median, share
    > 0, Spearman with modern prevalence). D modern guard vs Audubon (1967-86 -> 2005-24). E held-out by era (Spearman,
    AUC; INFLATED for V1-V3 in every era because the covariate uses the same list -- secondary). F scenario realism
    (non-zero count and zero share, degraded vs undegraded, beside real pre-1940 vs post-1966 at <= 6 counters).
decision (per variant):
  SENSITIVITY: a mechanism discriminates only if V0's species |bias| median there is >= 0.30; a non-discriminating
  mechanism is reported and not used for the rule.
  EARLY-USABLE iff ALL of
   (i) pseudo-early, every discriminating mechanism: species |bias| median <= 0.15 AND species corr >= 0.8 AND stratum
       corr (centred) >= 0.5;
   (ii) modern guard (D): species corr >= 0.8 AND stratum corr centred >= 0.6 (E064's C-a bars);
   (iii) early split-half reliability (B, centred) >= 0.5 on >= 100 stratum x species pairs (fewer: "not estimable",
       which blocks early use at stratum level but not at species level).
  Among usable variants the one with the smallest mean |bias| over discriminating mechanisms is preferred. V0 is
  expected to fail (i); if it passes, the pseudo-early test is not sensitive and is reported as such.
  None usable -> report which criterion fails and why; next angles, in order: early use restricted to counts of high
  relative completeness; community-level coverage standardization (Chao & Jost) for the community target directly
  (plans/raw_surveys.md section 7), which needs no per-species index.
known limits (the test can be passed and the early index still be wrong):
- the pseudo-early copies are MODERN circles, observers and identification skill with lists removed; early counts also
  differed in area, duration and where circles were -- (i) tests the correction's statistics, not every early cause;
- REAL TURNOVER READ AS INCOMPLETENESS: benchmark sets are modern; a species that truly increased since the 1930s
  lowers early "completeness" at circles where it is now common, so the covariate can absorb real community-wide change
  (leave-one-out protects only the target species). The pseudo-early test adds no real turnover and cannot see this.
  C is the only (weak) window on it. Untested -> any early use carries this caveat until tested;
- borrowed references for early-only circles are not reproduced by the pseudo-early copies (their circles are mostly
  modern-referenced);
- early covariate values lie far outside the modern range (median relative logit benchmark completeness -3.3 before
  1940 vs sd ~1.1 overall): the correction EXTRAPOLATES a slope fitted mostly on modern variation. (i) tests exactly
  that extrapolation at the 1920-39 level, not the 1901-19 level (0.14).
considerations: M29 (curators' own limits: Audubon starts in 1966); M28 (one yardstick per claim: pseudo-early recovery
is the yardstick for bias, split-half for noise); M2/M13 (point gates with their n; a skeptic round before any finding);
G11 (season: winter communities, which this would supply for the early era); no House Finch (absent from the species
set by construction); sealed species never read.

SKEPTIC ROUND (M13; computed after the decision, from the same fits):
- SCALE. V3's index is a mean detection probability, whose true species changes are HALF the scale of the count index
  (sd of true species change 0.27 vs 0.53-0.58; median |true| 0.22 vs 0.42-0.54). The |bias| bar (0.15) was written in
  count-index units. Relative to each variant's own spread, median |bias| / sd(true) is: thin V0 1.16, V1 0.60, V2 0.52,
  V3 0.35; omit V0 0.44, V1 0.25, V2 0.52, V3 0.41. At a scale-matched bar (0.15 x 0.27/0.58 ~ 0.07) V3 would fail
  narrowly (0.09 thin, 0.11 omit). Scale-free evidence still favours V3 under thinning (species corr 0.90 vs <= 0.69;
  relative bias 0.35 vs >= 0.52); under omission V1 is best and V3 barely beats no correction in relative terms.
- B is marginal (0.52 vs bar 0.50; modern 0.57 -- early reliability is near modern, so noise is not the binding limit).
- C IS UNEXPLAINED. Every variant has most species DECLINING from 1910-39 to 1950-65 (V3 median -0.15, 16% rising), and
  rarer species declining more (Spearman with prevalence +0.56 to +0.67). Incomplete early lists predict the OPPOSITE
  (a spurious rise, rare species most), which the pseudo-early copies do show (V0 +0.67). So real early counts carry an
  effect the pseudo-early mechanisms do not reproduce. Candidates (unknown, cheapest test first): selective early
  reporting of NOTABLE species (rare birds listed, common ones skipped -- inflating rare species early); counters
  understating early per-counter effort (long solo days), which V0's effort term would turn into high early indices;
  real mid-century declines; where early circles were.
READING (established / likely / unknown). Established: counters-only (V0) is biased by early-like incompleteness (+0.67
under thinning); list-length and benchmark covariates in a COUNT model correct the mean but not the species ranking
under thinning; the DETECTION model with benchmark completeness (V3) keeps the species ranking under both mechanisms.
Likely: V3 is the right family for pre-1940 CBC -- counts carry the effort-scale damage that detection sidesteps. Not
established: that V3's early indices are right. The pre-registered rule says usable; the skeptic round says the margin
is scale-dependent, and C shows a real-data pattern no tested mechanism explains. No early use of V3 until C is
understood. Untested still: real turnover read as incompleteness (M30), borrowed references, the 1901-19 level (0.14).
