CORRECTION (2026-10-15, after a user question): the design text above says a list's "own effort and completeness are
shared by every species on it and cancel". That is exact only for INDIVIDUAL records (each record a bird drawn in
proportion to abundance x detectability). With PRESENCES (each species at most once per list) a common species is listed
almost regardless of effort while a rare one is listed more as effort grows, so the species SHARE of a list's presences
shifts with completeness (e.g. listing rates 100% / 5% on a short list vs 100% / 50% on a long one move the rare
species' share from ~5% to ~33% with no change in the birds). The softmax normalizer, which any presence-only
likelihood needs (else predicting every species everywhere is optimal), turns that shift into relative information,
and time-constant species offsets cannot absorb CBC's rising completeness -- so the term can read it as community
change. No false positives or explicit absences are needed for this. Tested in E078 (species-specific completeness
slopes); a saturating detection model (E070's cloglog form) is the other standard remedy.
