SKEPTIC NOTE (after the reading): seed 2's control has an unusually high top-tercile k (0.82 vs 0.52 seed 1, 0.62 seed
3), so any perturbation of seed 2 tends to lower it (regression to the mean). Against the MEAN of the three control
seeds (0.65), top-tercile k: CBC head 0.56 / 0.64, eBird 0.61 / 0.74, both 0.62 / 0.67, learned presence 0.34 / 0.59 --
heads at the control level, presence below it. So the head arms' seed-2 top-tercile failures are likely the control's
luck, not harm; the presence harm stands under either baseline.
WHY (likely): a head with its own similarity contract lets each source keep its own structure; the shared z only
needs to make that structure linearly readable, which it does without changing the breeding geometry -- so neither
harm nor much transfer. Presence reads winter lists through the breeding readout, so fitting them moves z itself:
transfer to early lists, and harm to out-of-community breeding change.
IMPLICATION (for the survey): the trade-off is sharp -- winter information either enters the breeding space (helps early
winter lists, harms sedentary breeding change) or stays in its own head (no harm, no early gain). eBird / both heads
show small spatial gains (+0.01-0.015) and above-noise all-cell sedentary gains (both heads) -- the candidates worth
more seeds before any Stage 2.
