# E028 Is "damped persistence" habitat, or a change of observer?
hypotheses: [O1, B5, B2, E1]     parents: [E006, E021]     eval_version: pre-v1     cost: rung 0, ~2 min cpu
pre-registered: 2026-10-09 ~23:20, before the script was run.

question: on trained cells the corrected suite's best baseline is the spacetime_sum GP, whose fit degenerated into
per-cell damped persistence: a cell's deviation from the regional surface fades with a ~58-year timescale, so a backcast
pulls each cell toward its region (E006: TIME change skill +0.12 vs no change; DESK, which cannot do this, -0.008). Is
that fading real (habitat deviations are transient) or observers (a modern observer's personal deviation was not there
under the previous observer; E021: ~29% of change energy is observer turnover)? The answer decides whether "DESK loses
to the spacetime GP on trained cells" is a habitat failure, and whether a damped cell residual (B5) belongs in the
downstream model.

design: research/exp/persistence_observer.py on the gp_species_base cache. Within the modern era (2005-14 vs 2016-25),
cells classed by observer continuity (unchanged / partial / replaced). Per dev species: epoch-1 deviation from a
leave-cell-out Gaussian (150 km) regional surface, measured on one ABBA half; change = epoch 2 minus the OTHER half (so
noise does not create regression to the mean; a departing observer's effect still can). Pooled slope of change on
deviation per class, 6x6 block bootstrap; damped-persistence skill vs no change with the damping fitted on the other
classes.
predictions:
- observer-driven: slope(replaced) - slope(unchanged) < -0.10 with CI below 0, and slope(unchanged) >= -0.10.
- habitat-driven: the difference's CI spans 0 and both slopes are negative, near the -0.17 that a 58-year timescale
  implies over the ~11 years between epoch centres.
decision rule: observer-driven -> the trained-cell advantage of damped persistence is mostly observer turnover; "DESK
loses to the spacetime GP" on trained cells is not a habitat failure, and scoring needs the observer-adjusted target
(A14) before any trained-cell comparison is quoted. Habitat-driven -> transient local deviations are real; a damped
cell residual in the readout (B5) becomes a downstream candidate, and DESK's z.z' persistence (B2) is a real defect for
backcasting. Mixed -> report both shares.

Pre-registered reading: MIXED (the observer prediction misses its -0.10 threshold by 0.002; the habitat prediction

other half (-0.08 per ~11 years, where the same observers counted) is real decadal transience of local deviations --
or slow drift within observers, which this cannot separate. Over 11 years, -0.084 is a ~120-year e-folding time if
it compounds, slower than the spacetime_sum fit's 58 years, whose fit mixes in the observer turnover across 40 years.
Consequences: (1) on trained cells, the spacetime GP's advantage over DESK is partly observer turnover and partly a
real effect DESK cannot represent; the trained-cell comparison should not be quoted without the observer split.
(2) local deviations are partly transient, so a backcast should pull a cell's modern idiosyncrasy toward its region by
an amount that grows with reach (B5); a readout whose kernel treats a cell's past as its present (B2) gets this wrong.
note: both slopes are attenuated by the reliability of the half-epoch deviation (its own noise is in the denominator),
so the true fading rates are larger in magnitude than measured; the observer / non-observer split (~1:1) is not
affected by that attenuation.
