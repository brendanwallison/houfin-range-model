## What enters the likelihood

Verified positive count-day CBC records supply occurrence constraints. Pre-1940 positives are thinned to one per coarse cell/decade (81 constraints); the later arm also includes one per cell/year for 1951â€“1965 (1,147 total presence constraints including the historical 81). Count magnitudes and individual nondetections do not enter as CBC count/detection data. Actual survey dates align December y and January y+1 to summer y. The first neighboring-cell record discussed previously as CBC year 1953 was observed December 28, 1952, hence enters model year 1952.

Presence means potential accessible population must be non-negligible, not that CBC count equals population. The positive spatial operator takes the elementwise maximum/union of the existing local-circle footprint and possible 300 km southwest seasonal-source support. This is a fixed availability allowance, not a fitted migrant fraction or a new dynamic movement process. It deliberately weakens the implication that a winter occurrence proves permanent occupation of that exact breeding cell. Source weights are not interpreted as an absolute count conversion.

The zero penalty is the existing NB2 zero-event form `k log(1+N/k)`; the positive penalty is its censored event `-log(1-exp(-k log(1+N/k)))`. N is annual total population on the model's route-count scale. Calibration k is fixed at 0.43724203275856455, the accepted no-CBC seed41 reference BBS dispersion, for continuity with existing pseudozeros. There is no fitted CBC source scale, effort coefficient, circle effect or additional ecological scalar. This is an explicitly calibrated working range constraint, not a reconstructed generative CBC observation model or an independent abundance census. The fixed BBS-derived calibration is an empirical choice requiring sensitivity if conclusions depend on it.

## Range geometry and coverage

The pre-1940 native envelope pools verified pre-introduction CBC presences and adds the inherited conservative 700 km buffer. It is checked against the inherited native seed support: no seeded native cell falls outside it. Inside the envelope, unobserved cells remain unconstrained; neither occupancy nor absence is filled into its interior.

The later arm retains this native component and adds a separate introduced eastern component, never one hull across the continent. Eastern occurrence records are selected geographically (east of 85W, latitude at least 33N) and accumulated only through the actual observation year. The introduced envelope uses an explicitly conditional 330 km allowance to test finer invasion extent than the 700 km native envelope. This is not a proven maximum dispersal distance or an exact observed invasion boundary. It does not alone enforce no spread outside the release cell in 1951. Initialization already confines the injected pulse to its inherited release footprint before the annual transition; the exterior constraints supervise subsequent broad extent. Stronger local-front statements would require separately justified evidence and a buffer sensitivity.

Neither component creates new CBC-derived absences beyond the raw extract's 125Wâ€“66W/24Nâ€“53N domain. Outside that box the full inherited pseudozero footprint is retained as the historical assumption. The first unqueued draft lacked this guard; the sanity check caught 74 newly excluded out-of-domain cells. Those initial staged sources remain unqueued for provenance.

Coverage uses recorded CBC circle locations and decades, not imputed hours. A cell receives relative weight `1+log(1+distinct circle/decade units within 216 km)`. The nonzero baseline represents historical range knowledge where CBC coverage is poor; it is not claimed to be survey evidence. Pre-1940 coverage pools 1902â€“1939. Later coverage uses up to the preceding three years. Weights are normalized to the old active annual pseudozero budget, so changing grid density does not arbitrarily multiply evidence.

## Replacement and sanity check

All 68,932 inherited pre-1940 pseudozero rows remain in exported arrays for provenance but their likelihood weights are set to zero. Shape and route/count checks prevent masking real BBS records. Full-data checks verify exactly zero old pseudozero weight remaining. Actual BBS and NestWatch data are unchanged.

The old active weight is 60,610, not 68,932, because observation masks remove some pseudozero sites. The replacement pre-1940 constraint has exactly 60,610 total weight, spread over 2,107 cells. The original full cached footprint contains 1,961 cells before subsampling/masking; the derived geometry shares all 1,961 and adds 146. Only 1,595 original sites had active weight.

The original zero likelihood also has density-dependent dispersion: `c(mu)=k*max(mu,.001)^p`, with reference p=0.859118. The replacement fixes dispersion at k. Corrected matched-density curves include this difference and the old census's phi-weighted young. With young fraction .5, replacement losses are about 23% stronger at total .001, 36% stronger at .1, and 11% weaker at 1 route-scale population. Earlier constant-dispersion curves omitted this original term and are superseded; the queued likelihoods are unchanged.

The exact matched-trajectory calculation already used the original per-observation dispersion and remains valid: old absence loss is 7.462 and derived loss 81.838. Derived loss comprises 5.389 at originally active sites, 1.502 in the old footprint but not actively weighted, and 74.947 in genuinely added geographic cells. Added historical presence loss is 139.795. The actual stronger pressure is primarily geographic, despite identical total weight. It must not be described as an elevenfold uniform increase in per-cell strength.

The [complete comparison](HISTORICAL_PSEUDOZERO_COMPARISON_20261102.md) includes East/Great Plains/West/other support and weight, loss/gradient curves across densities and age mixes, machine-readable values and a figure. This comparison is conditional on the common reference point; the original learned dispersion can differ across fitted versions.

## Validation and execution

Two new numerical tests check the NB2 zero/censoring probabilities, finite first/second derivatives and invariance when quadrature weights are split across duplicate points. Both full-data model variants have finite objective and gradients for adult/juvenile survival, fecundity, capacity and introduced abundance. Diagnostic trajectory replay error is zero and BIDE residual below 6e-16. Old pseudozero weight is zero in both replacement models.

Methodological basis: presence/pseudoabsence selection and weighting follow established SDM practice, with choices made explicit rather than treating them as measured detection probabilities ([Barbet-Massin et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2041-210X.2011.00172.x)). Other-species survey locations help characterize collection coverage ([Phillips et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1890/07-2153.1)). Presence-only data do not identify absolute abundance without additional information ([Fithian et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12242)). These references support the components; the historical geometry, seasonal allowance and fixed constraint calibration remain project-specific assumptions.
