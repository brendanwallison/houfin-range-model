# House Finch fit: pathology inventory and configuration-selection checklist

Compiled 2026-10-31 from the age-model standing considerations and historical experiments, the October 26 demographic synthesis, the 54 km ladder and dispersal synthesis, and the fecundity scoping. This is a list of previously observed failure patterns and structural concerns. They do not all occur in every configuration. Numerical examples belong to their documented bases and life cycles, not to a single current fit.

**Selection objective:** find a reasonably parsimonious configuration that reduces the joint set of biological and inferential pathologies while retaining useful predictive performance. A slightly better likelihood, successful recovery of one simulated parameter, or elimination of one symptom does not by itself select the configuration. Conversely, incomplete parameter recovery does not erase a biological or predictive improvement.

## Primary biological readouts

| ID | Previously identified pattern | What makes it concerning | What to compare across candidates |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Large inferred historical improvement in habitat-driven growth**, especially the early eastern sink becoming a modern source | The population model can fit invasion timing by making the historical East broadly unsuitable and later opening it. Fixed-Z replay showed refinement amplifying this change about fivefold in an older split-readout fit. Real habitat change is possible; the concern is an exaggerated change serving as a timing mechanism. | Regional lambda trajectories, suitable-area maps, source/sink transitions, and agreement with independently learned habitat change; compare the same life-cycle definition. |
| P2 | **Expansion intrinsically too fast** | With habitat frozen, release-only fronts often arrive roughly 26–30 years early; fitted fronts can still be several years early. Habitat gating may conceal a growth/dispersal mismatch rather than repair it. | Observed versus fitted arrival/establishment curves and frozen-habitat counterfactuals, using more than one detection threshold. |
| P3 | **High potential population growth, with or without inflated fecundity** | Large potential lambda can be offset by density dependence, Allee suppression or movement mortality, leaving fitted abundance and realized replacement near equilibrium. This is an aggregate-growth pathology even if individual vital rates are weakly identified or appear plausible. Older fits reported occupied-habitat lambda medians around 2.5–3 and some established-cell values around 5; those examples span different life cycles and are not universal cutoffs. | Report potential-lambda levels, distributions and upper tails at identical East/Great Plains/West locations and eras, on fixed observed-cell and all-land support. For the current yearling life cycle use Sa+Sj*Fmax before density, Allee and movement effects; do not substitute the older delayed-breeding eigenvalue. Evaluate actual-state replacement, Runge contribution and growth at common imposed densities alongside it, identifying what suppresses the inflated potential growth. Assess Fmax and realized reproduction separately against NestWatch. The project term lambda inflation refers to exaggerated historical change (P1), not high growth levels (P3). Distinguish the two; better invasion timing or near-one realized growth does not by itself clear P3. |
| P4 | **Juvenile survival pushed to the adult-survival ceiling** | Occupied-habitat Sj/Sa around .98–1.00 in some fits, despite the expected first-year disadvantage. Enforcing Sj <= Sa prevents an inequality violation but does not prevent ceiling saturation. | Survival by age, habitat, region and era; share near the ratio ceiling; marked-bird consistency. Keep the survival of stayers separate from effective survival of movers. |
| P5 | **Moving demographic implausibility between parameters** | Constraining fecundity or changing the life cycle can shift the demand into juvenile survival, adult survival, habitat fields or founders. Historical variants reached Sa around .85–.95; some newer yearling variants instead lower adult survival relative to the BBL diagnostic. | Read F, Sa, Sj, founders and habitat together, in biological units. A less extreme F is not a complete fix if another rate becomes implausible. |
| P6 | **Large occupied native-west sink regions sustained by immigration** | Some configurations make a large fraction of the occupied native West unable to replace itself locally; earlier variants reached roughly 60–77% of western land as fundamental sinks, while simpler shared-field ladder fits reduced but did not eliminate the pattern. | Abundance-weighted sink share, actual observations in sink cells, local replacement deficits and immigrant contributions. Sinks are legitimate biology; their prevalence and required subsidy need evidence. Do not require every western cell to be a source. |
| P7 | **Carrying capacity collapses to an absence switch** | Very small K, notably in the Plains in several variants, can replace an Allee barrier or demographic limitation and cheaply suppress occupancy. Standing considerations explicitly distinguish implausibly cheap tiny K from genuinely possible high K. | Low-K area, geographic continuity, K at surveyed/occupied cells, and whether absence pressure moves from another term into K. Keep prior/observation-scale units consistent. |
| P8 | **Suitable habitat that cannot establish because of the Allee rule** | “Allee-dead” cells have positive post-establishment growth but fail to establish under the actual low-density dynamics. Stronger Allee settings can create abrupt die-or-explode systems rather than gradual spread. | Fundamental and realized growth together, establishment thresholds, Allee-dead share, and sensitivity to starting density. Counts may identify persistent negative effective growth without identifying whether low lambda or mate limitation caused it. |
| P9 | **Implausible introduction size or early release-cell history** | Founder pulses range from less than one bird on some native delayed-breeding fits to thousands on some strong-Allee arms. Founders buy off changes in movement or establishment. | Founder numbers under the explicit count-to-bird conversion, early CBC detections/nondetections, release-cell population through time and neighboring-cell arrival. Treat conversion/detection uncertainty explicitly. |

## Dispersal and habitat escape routes observed in particular variants

| ID | Pattern | Why it can give a deceptively good fit | Diagnostic |
|---|---|---|---|
| P10 | **Excessively long-tailed juvenile movement** | Learned band weights put about 60% of juvenile movement beyond 483 km and raise mean distance to roughly 625 km in one arm. Redistribution into sparse/sink habitat can act like an implicit loss and reduce the required fecundity without fixing front speed. | Band masses, realized distances, self-cell retention, immigration subsidies and season-filtered banding evidence, with encounter bias acknowledged. |
| P11 | **Sparse-cell exodus followed by artificial regathering** | Older learned-departure/settlement combinations empty sparse populations and gather them back into favorable cells. The pair fits well even though the two mechanisms are compensating for one another. | Departure curves over N/K, settlement allocation and local population changes before/after each operation. Historical examples had departure falling from ~87% at zero density to ~1% near N/K=.8. |
| P12 | **A free journey-survival field replaces juvenile biology** | With most juveniles departing in established cells, Q is effectively their annual survival. Loosely decoupling Q from Sj allowed very high journey survival while resident juvenile survival collapsed. This becomes a density-dependent survival escape route, not merely a travel penalty. | Departure-weighted juvenile survival, resident versus mover survival, Q intercept/slopes and consistency of the marked-bird transition. |
| P13 | **Opposing or highly flexible demographic projections on the same habitat basis** | Earlier private response fields could raise fecundity while lowering juvenile survival in compensating directions. Separate spatial/temporal readouts could make an adult temporal vector almost orthogonal to its spatial vector, allowing historical growth patterns weakly related to the fitted spatial ecology. | Coefficient directions/norms, maps of each rate and temporal extrapolation. A single shared field removes some flexibility but has not by itself removed P1–P5. |
| P14 | **Amplitude, coefficient-norm and observation-effect scale inflation** | Joint MAP fitting of hierarchical scales lets amplitudes and latent coefficients trade off; old free route-effect SDs reached ~7.5. Fixing a habitat amplitude alone can simply inflate the underlying field vector instead. | Effective field magnitude and coefficient norm, not only the named amplitude; route/observer effects and held-out behavior. This is an inferential parameterization concern as well as a parsimony concern. |
| P15 | **Flexible trends/corrections absorb the biological task** | Larger K-trend budgets, free regional time corrections, state warps or process-noise terms can repair particular residual patterns while leaving demographic contortions intact or bypassing the expansion mechanism. | Correction magnitude and spatial support, implied K changes, mechanistic counterfactuals and predictive transfer. These are useful diagnostics; their fit gain alone does not justify a final mechanism. |

## Structural and numerical concerns that qualify the inventory

- **Delayed first breeding:** the old life cycle can inflate apparent fecundity demand. Yearling breeding is a biologically motivated structural alternative, but historical switches also moved pressure into other terms. Evaluate the complete configuration, not the switch in isolation.
- **Absolute versus relative Allee effects:** the standing biological preference is absolute mate-finding density. Relative-to-K Allee laws were diagnostic alternatives and remove some Allee-dead behavior by construction; that is not independent evidence that the biological problem is solved. Mark this assumption explicitly in every candidate.
- **Path averaging dilutes barriers:** sigmoid of average path habitat is not compounded survival across a heterogeneous journey. Land conditioning also removes water from the habitat average. These are known approximation limits in the dispersal structure, not proof of a particular fitted barrier. Compounding is a candidate realism improvement even when its magnitude is weakly identified.
- **Migration versus permanent dispersal:** all-season recoveries mix the two, potentially inflating a kernel intended to describe changes of breeding location. The seasonal branch is a future structural test, not part of the current configuration search.
- **Threshold sensitivity and numerical artifacts:** sharp establishment transitions can produce large timing shifts under tiny parameter perturbations. Float32 and float64 can follow different invasion histories. Older floors, point-discretized kernels at coarser grids and low-land-support path values are additional implementation hazards. Some were fixed; require explicit provenance rather than assuming every historical result is current. A numerical convergence flag is necessary but not biological validation.
- **Broad compensation/weak identification:** similar bird-count scores can accompany very different demographic histories. Synthetic recovery and deliberately wrong auxiliary-data assumptions measure that uncertainty. They qualify confidence and guide design; they are not a standalone veto on useful, parsimonious structural changes or additional observations.

## How to choose the configuration

**HMC feasibility is an additional computational criterion, separate from P1–P15.** The implemented audit measures the full unconstrained posterior's local curvature, conditional-prior-standardized spectrum estimates, finite-difference/roughness behavior and variation near the starting point. Actual bounded NUTS pilots compare diagonal and block-dense ecological metrics while retaining observer nuisance parameters. Compare divergences, energy behavior, step sizes, trajectory saturation and ESS per leapfrog/wall time; neither model size nor a MAP Hessian ratio alone establishes sampler suitability. Current execution: HMC_DIAGNOSTIC_RESULTS_20261101.md. Full inference additionally needs longer dispersed chains, rank-normalized bulk/tail diagnostics and Monte Carlo error for the biological quantities of interest.

For each candidate, keep one compact scorecard with:

1. **Structure and complexity:** breeding life cycle, Allee law, shared/private habitat fields, departure/settlement, kernel choice, observation model, auxiliary datasets; count free spatial/temporal fields as well as scalar parameters.
2. **Demography:** realized offspring per breeder/pair, Fmax, Sa/Sj and ratio saturation, low-density growth; compare under the correct life cycle and census timing.
3. **Maps and history:** early-East suitability, magnitude/pattern of historical change, western immigration dependence, low-K/Allee-dead regions, and invasion timing.
4. **Movement and founders:** departure curves, distance distribution, path survival, founder scale and early CBC history.
5. **Prediction and robustness:** matched held-out scores with paired uncertainty, seed/initialization sensitivity, alternative DESK sensitivity and numerical validity.

Prefer a simpler configuration that meaningfully reduces several concerns at an acceptable predictive cost. Retain tradeoffs explicitly rather than collapsing everything into one arbitrary weighted pathology score. Do not seek flat historical lambda, no sink cells or minimal movement as ends in themselves. The target is a credible explanation of the observed history with fewer compensating mechanisms.

Start from a small, interpretable structural baseline and add a mechanism when it improves realism or a named failure pattern sufficiently to justify its extra freedom. More observation data can improve this comparison without adding another biological field. Any claimed improvement should state which pathologies decreased, which increased, and which remain unresolved.
