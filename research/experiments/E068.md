# E068 Why do real early CBC counts show species DECLINING out of 1910-39, rare species most?
# (pre-registered 2026-10-13 ~19:45, before running; no smoke beyond the E065 code)
hypotheses: [CBC (bridge / complement)]     parents: [E065 (C), E066]
eval_version: pre-v1     cost: 2 fits x ~25 min cpu + ~3 min
why: E065's C check found, in every variant, most species declining from 1910-39 to 1950-65 (V0 median -0.36, 23%
rising; V3 -0.15, 16%), rarer species declining more (Spearman with modern prevalence +0.56 to +0.67). Incomplete
early lists predict the opposite (a spurious rise, rare species most), which the pseudo-early copies do produce (V0
+0.67 under thinning). Until this is understood, no early-era CBC index is used (E065 reading). Two candidates are
cheap to test; the user (2026-10-13) asked to keep testing.
(a) EFFORT EXTRAPOLATION -- early counts have median 2 field counters (modern 18); the effort term p[s] * log(E / mean E)
    is extrapolated far below the data's centre, and the index is read at MEAN effort. If p[s] is larger for rare
    species, their early indices are pushed up more -> a larger apparent decline afterwards.
    Test: per fit, Spearman of species change with p[s], and partial correlations (rank residuals) of change with p
    given prevalence and with prevalence given p; plus REFITS with one fixed exponent for every species (--p-fixed: V0
    at 0.73, E064's counters median; V3 at 0.32, its own median), where (a) cannot act.
(b) SELECTIVE (notable-first) REPORTING -- early lists naming the interesting species and skipping common ones.
    Test: in circles counted both before 1940 and from 1966, per list-length bin (5-14, 15-24, 25-39 species), each
    species' inclusion rate on early vs modern lists of the same length (modern weighted to the early circle mix);
    Spearman of log(early/modern) with modern prevalence. Notable-first reporting predicts rare species over-included,
    most strongly on SHORT lists; a real decline of rare species predicts it in every bin alike.
reading (fixed now; research/exp/e068_early_decline.py):
- (a) SUPPORTED if in V0 |Spearman(change, p)| >= 0.5 AND the fixed-p V0 refit cuts Spearman(change, prevalence) by at
  least half (<= +0.33) AND moves the median change toward 0 by >= 0.15. Partly: one of the two refit conditions.
- (b) SUPPORTED if Spearman(log ratio, prevalence) <= -0.3 on the shortest bin AND at least 0.2 more negative there than
  on the longest bin. If all bins are within 0.1 of each other and <= -0.3: a prevalence-related difference that does
  not depend on list completeness -> real change or a place effect, not reporting.
- Neither: the cause stays unknown; next candidates are where early circles were (place) and real mid-century change.
decision: (a) supported -> the effort exponent must not be extrapolated to early effort (fix or shrink p, or model
early effort per count as a latent), and E065 is re-read with that model before any early use. (b) supported -> early
lists are presence-biased toward notable species: absences AND relative inclusion are unreliable, so early CBC can enter
only as presences conditioned within list (the user's presence-only route, E069 design) or not at all.
considerations: M29 (curators' limits), M30 (site-relative completeness), M2/M13 (point values with n; skeptic round),
no House Finch (not in the species set), sealed species never read.

## E068b Selective reporting, higher power (pre-registered 2026-10-13 ~20:05, before running)
design: within EARLY (< 1940) lists only, and separately within MODERN (>= 1966) lists of the same circles, a
species' inclusion as a function of log list length: per species, logistic slope b_s of inclusion on centred log list
length with circle fixed effects (approximated by within-circle centring of list length). Under random under-sampling
(less effort, thinning or omission) every species' inclusion rises with list length and rare species rise most
(they are added last) -> slope DEcreases with prevalence in both eras. Under notable-first reporting, early rare
species are listed even on short lists -> in the EARLY era their slopes are FLATTER than in the modern era.
statistic: d_s = b_s(early) - b_s(modern), slopes on the logit scale (linear-probability slope / pbar(1 - pbar),
pbar = the species' inclusion rate in that era); Spearman over species of d_s with modern prevalence, species with >= 30
early inclusions; bootstrap over circles (500 resamples). A LEVEL change of a species (real or not) does not change how
its inclusion depends on list length, so real decline does not confound d_s.
reading: notable-first predicts rare species FLATTER early relative to modern and common species not -> d_s rises with
prevalence -> POSITIVE Spearman. SUPPORTED if Spearman >= +0.3 with the 95% bootstrap interval excluding 0; NOT
supported if the interval covers 0 or the sign is negative. Medians of b_s per era are reported for context.
research/exp/e068b_reporting_slopes.py.

(3) early MISIDENTIFICATION, which inflates rare species early (false positives) and would also produce this pattern;
(4) a model artifact specific to sparse early strata (few circles per stratum-year). Unknown; no early-era index is used.

## E068c Is the early decline a PLACE effect? Persistent circles only (pre-registered 2026-10-13 ~20:25, before running)
design: refit V0 and V3 (E065 settings) on circles with >= 5 counts in 1910-1939 AND >= 5 in 1950-1965 (--persistent 5;
the long-running-circle approach of Saunders et al. 2022), index over fitted circles only (E065 erratum fix); C statistics
by e068_early_decline.py. If the decline comes from WHERE counts were made in each window (early circles in different
places or habitats than mid-century ones; circle relocations), it should shrink when the same circles carry both windows.
reading: PLACE SUPPORTED if, for V0, the median change moves toward 0 by >= 0.15 AND Spearman(change, prevalence) falls
to <= +0.33 (half of +0.67). If the median stays within 0.10 of the all-circle value: the decline is NOT a circle-mix
effect -> real change, early misidentification or a sparse-stratum artifact remain. In between: report. The number of
persistent circles is reported (if < 50, the test is underpowered and reported as such).
ALSO (erratum, E065): V3 half-fits h0/h1 re-run under the index fix; E065 B re-read for V3 only.

WHY the species-wide part (likely). Most early circles do not span the eras (E065: only 34-46% of pre-1940 rows are in
circles with >= 5 modern counts). For a circle seen only early, its circle effect and the early stratum-year effect are
not separately identified; the ridge on circle effects (sd 3) pulls the circle toward the average and pushes its
richness into the stratum-year effect. If early-only circles sat in richer places -- Dunn et al. 2005 note early
circles clustered near population centres -- the early index is inflated and every species appears to decline. When
the same circles carry both windows, the decline almost vanishes (median -0.05 / -0.03).
WHY the prevalence pattern (unknown): real relative change (rare species declining, common ones increasing -- biotic
homogenization would look like this), early misidentification of rare species, or the reduced sample (128 circles).
E065 ERRATUM RE-READ: V3 split-half under the index fix: early centred 0.539 (was 0.520), modern 0.564 (was 0.570)
-> V3's (iii) still passes (>= 0.5), slightly more securely.
IMPLICATION for any early CBC index: use circles that SPAN the eras (as Saunders et al. 2022 did), or model early-only
circles' effects with a spatial prior (neighbouring circles) instead of a ridge toward the average; the pseudo-early
test (E065) could not see this, because its degraded counts were all in modern circles.
