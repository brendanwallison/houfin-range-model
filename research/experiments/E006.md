READING (UNVERIFIED):
1. The three concerns (DESK loses to the spacetime GP; DESK ~ no change on change; the oracle is weak), corrected suite: DESK is still slightly worse than no change on change (TIME and
   SPACE_TIME). The oracle (the true community, half-window) is ALSO slightly worse than no change, and DESK beats it
   on held-out cells: the loss is mostly downstream of DESK -- the community-to-species readout plus noise -- which is
   what E013 found from the other side. DESK "loses to the spacetime GP" only on TRAINED cells; on held-out cells the
   GP has nothing cell-specific to work with and does no better than DESK.
2. What wins on trained cells is DAMPED PERSISTENCE: predicting that a cell's modern deviation was smaller in the
   past. DESK cannot do this (no year input, no cell residual: its kernel z.z' treats a cell's past as its present,
   B2). But part or most of that win may be OBSERVER turnover rather than habitat: the 1966-75 and 2005-25 counts come
   from different observers in nearly every cell (E021: 81% even within 2005-25), so a modern observer-specific
   deviation is exactly the kind of thing that "was smaller in the past". Untested; E028 candidate: does damped
   persistence beat no change on within-modern change more where the observer changed than where it did not?
3. "No change" is not the strongest null on trained cells; a shrink-toward-the-regional-surface null is.
