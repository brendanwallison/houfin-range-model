# E031 Is a species' change shared with the species it lives with (habitat-like), or its own?
hypotheses: [P1, R5, E2, O1, B7]     parents: [E030, E013, E011, E021, E028]     eval_version: pre-v1     cost: rung 0
pre-registered: 2026-10-10 ~08:03 (the user's test, after they pointed out that removing each species' regional trend
first would wrongly assume habitat does not drive regional trends), before running.

question: the community's ~40-year change (1966-86 vs 2005-25) carries only ~5-7% of a graded species' change (E030).
Is the rest the species' own story (spread, disease, wintering grounds: the population model's job), or habitat change
the species shares with its particular associates that the community summary fails to carry? Habitat change should
move species that use the same habitat together; a species' own dynamics move it alone. Regional vs local cannot
separate them (habitat change is regional too); shared vs unique can.
design: research/exp/shared_change.py on the gp_species_base cache (graded = the 273 dev species; candidates = dev +
the 96 reference species; sealed species never read). Associates: the K = 5 species whose time-averaged abundance
(mean of the 1966-86 and 2005-25 epochs, so not chosen BY the change) tracks the target's most closely across
TRAINING cells. Statistic: noise-free centred correlation, on HELD-OUT cells, of the target's change with its
associates' mean standardized change (ABBA cross-halves, so one survey's shared weather/day effects cancel), pooled
over resolvable species; null: K random species (30 draws). Also on all cells; across species (does a species'
continent-wide mean change follow its associates'?); and the observer check: the same statistic for the 11-year
change 2005-14 -> 2016-25 in cells whose observers were unchanged vs replaced.
predictions / reading rule:
- mostly unique: associates' corr^2 < 0.10 and within ~0.05 of the random null -> the low 5-7% reflects species' own
  dynamics; a community-based habitat is not missing much shared signal.
- substantially shared: associates' corr^2 >= 0.15 and above random by >= 0.05 -> the community summary misses
  habitat-shared signal (E030 found 5-7%); look at which associates carry it (guilds) and why ESK does not.
- observers: if associates - random is similar in observer-unchanged and replaced cells, observer change is not what
  makes associates co-move; if it collapses where observers were unchanged, the sharing is mostly observation.
---- RESULTS (2026-10-10 ~08:05; job E031/shared-change-d22db828f447; UNVERIFIED) ----
K = 5 associates (median spatial corr of time-averaged abundance with the target 0.43). Noise-free centred corr of a
species' change with its associates' mean change | K random species (30 draws):
  40-year change, held-out cells (278 cells, 128 species): associates 0.290 (median per species 0.303) | random 0.058
    (p95 0.074) -> shared share 0.084 vs 0.003
  40-year change, all cells (1844, 145 species): 0.298 | 0.053 -> 0.089 vs 0.003
  across species (continent-wide mean change, 146 species): 0.321 | random -0.012 (p95 0.116)
  11-year change (2005-14 -> 2016-25): observers unchanged (565 cells) 0.247 | 0.043; replaced (500) 0.388 | 0.093
Pre-registered: neither branch exactly. "Mostly unique" fails its second clause (associates are 0.08 above the random
share, not within 0.05); "substantially shared" fails its first (0.084 < 0.15). Reading by the numbers:
- about 9 in 10 parts of a species' 40-year change pattern are NOT shared with the species it lives with -- its own
  story, as far as five close associates can tell;
- about 1 in 10 is shared, specifically with its associates (random species share ~0.3%), and the sharing survives
  where the same observer counted throughout (11 years: 0.25 vs 0.04); a change of observer adds to it (0.39 vs 0.09);
- the community summary already carries most of that shared part: E030's community readout reaches 5-7% where five
  associates reach 8-9% -- so ESK is not missing much shared (habitat-like) signal;
- species' continent-wide trends follow their associates' (0.32 vs ~0): shared drivers act at continental scale too,
  which a centred (place-specific) score does not see.
Consequence for E030: the low information is mostly species' own dynamics -- the population model's job -- not habitat
the community fails to carry. ESK's loss against no change in the score is the scaling and the noise. Caveats: five
associates and a linear mean are a lower bound on sharing; associates were picked by where species live, not by
known habitat guilds.
